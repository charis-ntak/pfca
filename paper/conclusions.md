# 5. Conclusions
This work introduced Pareto optimal fuzzy concept attribution, a post hoc explanation method for tabular black box models that attributes predictions to fuzzy concepts of correlated features, represents the uncertainty of every attribution as a trapezoidal fuzzy number with a sign confidence, a disagreement index and linguistic labels, and selects the explanation configuration from a Pareto front of fidelity loss, complexity and instability. The motivating observation was that feature level Shapley values split credit arbitrarily among correlated features, report no reliability and cannot be tuned toward the properties a user needs. The method was evaluated on a reduced version of a four phase study design, with synthetic ground truth, a public benchmark, a simulated questionnaire with known subscales and a sensitivity analysis, against feature level SHAP, bootstrapped SHAP, grouped SHAP, LIME and four ablations of the method, all computed from one shared pool of refitted models.

The results indicated that the concept level attribution is markedly more stable than feature level attribution, with a rank stability of [A_RS_PFCA] against [A_RS_SHAP] in the redundancy family and of 1.000 against 0.507 on the breast cancer dataset, that the attribution of a fixed concept partition is invariant to the duplication of a feature where feature level attributions change by about half of their magnitude, that the sign of a concept attribution is calibrated with expected calibration errors below 0.10 in every phase, and that the three objectives of the selection conflict so that the front never collapsed. The results also indicated two costs of the default knee point: the attribution recovery error against the synthetic truth was higher than that of feature level SHAP because the knee merged factors into about two concepts, and the fuzzy supports covered the truth and the replicate less often than their nominal level at 20 resamples. Of the three pre registered success criteria, the stability part of the first and the deletion part of the second were met, whereas the recovery error, the insertion area, the surrogate fidelity and the interval coverage were not, and these failures are reported as the pre registration requires. Beyond the numbers, the contribution is an explanation that states how reliable it is, which concepts the model classes disagree on, and which simpler or more faithful alternatives exist on the front.

The main limitations of this work are the following.
* First, the study was executed at a reduced scale, with two seeds per cell, 20 resamples and two model classes instead of the 50 seeds, 100 resamples and three classes of the full design, which limits the power of the mixed models and widens the gap between nominal and observed coverage; the configuration files of the full design are distributed with the code.
* Second, the benchmark phase used a single dataset because the OpenML downloads were not reachable from the execution environment, so the paired tests across datasets of the protocol could not be applied and the second success criterion rests on one dataset.
* Third, the questionnaire phase used a simulated questionnaire, since the real dataset is covered by an ethics approval that does not allow redistribution, so the substantive claim that the concept level reveals reliability differences between constructs is demonstrated on known rather than on observed constructs.
* Fourth, the expert judgment study of Phase D was not conducted, so the interpretability of the linguistic outputs for domain experts remains an assumption supported only by the design of the labels.
* Fifth, the coverage of the true attribution is bounded by the bias of the reference model, which no resampling scheme can remove, so the fuzzy numbers should be read as statements about the estimator rather than about the truth.

Future research should run the full design on the public benchmarks and on the real questionnaire, with the expert study of Phase D, and should test whether a calibrated number of resamples or a knee criterion that accounts for the number of factors expected from the domain, such as the hyperplane criterion, closes the gap between the knee explanation and the generating structure. The integration of conditional Shapley estimators into the pool could reduce the bias of the attribution under dependence, and the development of a formal treatment of the selection membership as a possibility distribution over explanations may connect the two layers of fuzziness that the method keeps separate.
